## 1. Summary

Ten validators in ten GCP regions plus one observer ran telcoin-network 067639def (branch `bench/main-fixes-2026-11`: origin/main 4bf4604d2, the nine bench-4 observability commits and 29 topic commits for F1, F3, the engine (E1-E5), the land-path timing lines, batch-wake and cert-requeue), on bench 4's fleet, consensus parameters and chaos plan, from one coordinated start (15:23:30Z, 11/11, spread 0 s).
New in the harness: a light-load arm of three blasts before the load (8 and 16 senders per generator at window 16, then 600 senders at window 4) and a 1-in-100 export of the generators' landings.
No node panicked or logged a database error.
Warn and error lines fell 71 % (77,168 against 266,118), the engine's log from 1,163 to 5 MB/min per validator, and the teardown from 6h58m in five attempts to 35 min in one.

Throughput fell: 3,448.7 tx/s with all generators running against 4,578.0 in r20261105-0803 (bench 4, −24.7 %) and 4,037.2 in r20261103-1922 (bench 3, −14.6 %); best 60 s 4,939.2; 9.29 M landed; land p50 / p95 2.81 / 14.40 s; time to seen (the sender's scanner reads the receipt) p50 / p95 14.18 / 53.39 s against 12.92 / 29.78 s.
At light load a transaction lands at p50 0.25 s by the whole-second block timestamp and its sender sees it at p50 1.50-1.63 s (A1/kpis_table.md, A6/stages.md).
What the three fixes did, one line each:
- F1 (commit no longer waits for execution) works. No healthy validator was demoted (bench 4: two), although v01's
  execution fell 62 rounds behind its commit, and no generator was answered `-32603 validator is inactive` before the
  kill (A3, A1).
- F3 (one rejoin, at the network head) works. The killed validator rejoined once, mid-epoch, held, and had its own
  certificate committed 1.85 s after the promotion (bench 4: 13 promotions, 12 failed). It still needed 28 min from the
  kill: it had to replay 165 outputs while the network ran at its execution ceiling, so the 2-min target was not met (A3).
- F2 (the engine) halved the execute cost (165 → 87.4 µs/tx), but the whole task per transaction rose 34 % (215 →
  288.6 µs/tx) because the MDBX commit fsync went from about 0.32 to 1.04 s per output. The logical writes per
  transaction match bench 4's; the device wrote 4.3 times the bytes. Bench 3 had the same high-write signature, so
  bench 4 is the outlier. The cause is below the engine and is not settled by this run (A4n).

Two new systemic findings:
1. An F1-1 side effect starved the three fastest validators at vote time. A proposer names its own newest executed
   block and a voter signs only once it has executed that block. With the commit no longer waiting for execution,
   Tokyo, Hong Kong and Mumbai ran 3-11 s ahead of the other seven, whose execution reached each named block 3-4 s
   after the proposal, while a header lives about 0.86 s before the next one cancels it. The seven slower validators
   are a quorum by themselves, so every round moved on without the three. Before the kill they certified 0.2 %, 0.0 %
   and 16.3 % of their own headers, and their users landed nothing for 787, 817 and 487 s with no RPC error (A2, A1,
   A6). F3-6's 1-s bound is where the refusal shows, not its cause; the fix is for proposers to name a block a quorum
   has already executed (finding 4).
2. Time to land is now measured stage by stage. At light load the floor is 0.65 s to the creation of the leader header
   that commits the transaction, 1.25 s to the commit and 1.50 s to seen, 0.5-0.7 s better than predicted; the step from
   the leader's creation to its commit is 40 % of it. At saturation the engine queue holds 9.6 of the 14.9 s, and
   Little's law holds: four times the transactions in flight gave 13 % less throughput and 5.2 times the latency (A6).

The D3 triage (`benchmark/private/bench5-r20261107-1504/D3/triage.md`) confirmed three more systemic findings (§4,
findings 9-11). Accepted transactions are silently lost until the client resends them after 300 s, and the resends are
included twice: one gap, a sealed but uncommitted transaction is invisible to the pool and the RPC; it is the run's
land p99 of 403 s. A loaded epoch close waits for the seventh node to execute up to the closing output (22.6- and
11.0-s gaps, both loaded epoch records at exactly 7 of 10). And execution lag feeds itself: a deeper engine queue makes
`finish` and the signer job slower, so which validators are slow is a state, not hardware.

The main protocol recommendation is A6's re-ranked list of levers (finding 5): bound what is in flight at the node by the execution backlog (−10 to −12 s of saturated p50, no new capacity); pipeline the persist (×1.42 throughput, −4.4 s of saturated p50); give every proposer a short parent grace so London's and the Netherlands' certificates are included (their users' seen p50 14.5-18.7 s → 2 s or less at light load, for +0.07 to +0.19 s on the fleet floor); have proposers name an execution block a quorum already holds (finding 4); then #532 fast direct commit (−0.15 to −0.35 s off the light-load floor, the largest pure protocol cut).

Verdict: F1 and F3 hold as measured, and F3-6 stays.
F1-1 needs a proposer-side companion before bench 6: name an execution block a quorum already holds, so the fastest validators are not refused (finding 4).
Before any throughput claim, find out what changed the MDBX write volume between bench 4 and bench 5: a short arm of the bench-4 image on the bench-6 fleet tells the build from the environment.

### 4. New, an F1-1 side effect exposed at vote time: the three fastest validators' headers were refused

A voter signs a header only once its own execution has reached the block the header names, and a proposer names its own newest executed block (`proposer.rs:379`).
F1-1 removed the commit's wait for execution, so under load the validators' executed heights spread out.
Tokyo, Hong Kong and Mumbai, the fastest executors, ran 1-6 outputs (about 25 blocks each, 3-11 s) ahead of the middle tier and named blocks the other seven had not executed.
Those seven refused, and at n = 10 they are a quorum by themselves, so every round advanced without the three.
F3-6 (cae41a400), the voter's 1-s bound on its wait, is where the refusal is reported, not its cause.
Source: `tasks/issues/bench5-execution-behind-vote-starvation.md` (issue text), which traced the mechanism in the code at 067639def, and A2.

Evidence, load with all ten alive, 17:41:32-17:54:44 (A2/certification.md "Far-validator starvation under load", A2/vote-path.md, A6/stages.md §7, A1/kpis.md):

| node | own header rounds certified | exec lag avg / max (rounds) | execute µs/tx | batches sealed | no own certificate for |
|---|---|---:|---:|---:|---|
| v04 Tokyo | 2 of 918 (0.2 %) | 6.4 / 12 | 84.1 | 94 | 787 s, 17:41:50.404 → 17:54:57.757 |
| v08 Hong Kong | 0 of 918 (0.0 %) | 6.5 / 12 | 84.5 | 31 | 817 s, then 226 s from 18:05:42 |
| v10 Mumbai | 149 of 918 (16.3 %) | 9.1 / 18 | 85.7 | 126 | 487 s, from 17:46:44.672 |
| v02, v06, v07, v09 | 99.6-99.9 % | 12.6-13.5 / 18-20 | 87.3-87.6 | 889-2,089 (all seven) | |
| v01, v03, v05 | 99.9-100 % | 22.2-30.7 / 38-48 | 93.9-97.1 | | |

The mechanism:
- A header lives one round. The certifier cancels the outstanding vote requests when the proposer hands it the next
  header (`certifier.rs:818`), on average every 0.86 s in this window (918 rounds in 792 s). The middle tier reached the
  named block 3.0-3.9 s after the proposal. Traced: v04 proposed round 1073 at 17:44:59.950 naming block 21,772; the
  round was cancelled at v04:17:45:01.349; v02 executed block 21,772 at 17:45:03.813. Bench 4's 5-s wait would still have
  voted after the cancel, so dropping F3-6 would not cure this and would bring back #1782.
- The voter checks the block in `handler.rs:1124-1169` and refuses after 1 s: `vote path timing … outcome=behind
  lock_ms=393 exec_wait_ms=1001 total_ms=1396` (v02:17:45:00.354); the requester logs v04:17:45:01.228 `recoverable vote
  error, retrying … header block 21772, local block 21719`. Each of the seven slower voters refused v04's headers
  537-683 times over the 918 rounds; v01 and v05 never voted for it. Only v08 (861 votes) and v10 (542) did: three with
  its own, against the seven it needs. Retries exist (a recoverable refusal is resent after 250 ms up to six times, and
  the certifier backs off 0-10 s) and cannot outrun a one-round header.
- The voter holds the author's lock across the wait, so refusals queue at one per second per author, including requests
  for headers cancelled seconds earlier (v01's lock waits 7.3-8.5 s). The queue does not cause the starvation; it delays
  a voter's first useful vote after it catches up.
- The seven slower validators name blocks everyone has executed, so they certify each other and the three's headers are
  cancelled every round (`header uncertified reason=canceled` on 916 of v04's 918 rounds).
- Why these three: a 2-4 % difference in execute cost (table) became a lead of 2-12 rounds within minutes, and finding 11
  shows the order is a state of the queue, not of the machines.
- L7 worked as built and could not help: the cancelled digests went into the next header 2,811 of 2,813 times, and that
  header was refused for the same reason. About 20 digests per node cycled through 296-368 headers and needed 560-640 s
  to commit; none was lost. With nothing certified, the three workers almost stopped sealing, so the generators' later
  transactions waited in the pool: accepted, no RPC error, no WARN line.
- The kill changed the arithmetic. With nine alive at most two certificates per round could be missing, and the slow
  tier was refusing nearly everything (v01 and v03 refused 14,082 votes after v05's restart), so rounds had to wait for
  a fast node's certificate. All three certified again 7-13 s after the kill (v10:17:54:51.596, v08:17:54:57.495,
  v04:17:54:57.757), before the epoch boundary, and then took turns: v04 fell to 1-21 own certificates a minute at
  18:00-18:03, v08 to none from 18:05:42 to 18:09:28 (generator 08's second silence, 227 s).
- Run totals of `votes_refused_execution_behind_total`: v01 14,785, v03 11,953, v05 5,744, v06 3,167, v09 2,725, v07
  2,558, v02 2,557, v10 2,220, v04 1,317, v08 588; 0 at idle and light load. `vote_timeouts_total{stage}` 0 everywhere,
  as F3-6 intended (A3/verdicts.md).

Why bench 4 did not show it: with the commit waiting for execution no validator could execute far ahead of the others; the slow executors were demoted instead (bench-4 F1), and the fast nodes certified (v04 99.7 %, v08 96.0 %, v10 87.0 %).
The mechanism is not regional: any validator whose execution runs more than about a round ahead of 2f others is starved for as long as the other 2f + 1 certify without it, up to f validators at a time.

Impact: three of ten generators landed nothing for 8-14 minutes with no error to tell them, and up to 28,800
transactions (the three generators' windows) sat accepted and waiting. The stall also set off the generators' 300-s
resends and the duplicate batches of finding 9.
The chain kept its rate, because the engine bound it either way.

What to change (issue text, "Bench-6 proposal").
Keep F3-6: without it the voter's wait is unbounded again (#1782), and the starvation stays.
The recommended fix is proposer-only (design D): the proposer names the (2f+1)-th highest block among its own tip and its parents' execution blocks, never above its own tip and never more than 8 outputs below it, resolved to its own number and hash.
At least 2f other voters already hold that block, so only the at most f slowest refuse, and the round does not need them.
No validity rule changes and no fork gate is needed, a mixed fleet works, and it can be cherry-picked for bench 6; the cost is a fork canary that checks a block up to 8 outputs old at saturation (none when the fleet is in step).
The companion (V) is voter-only: wait for execution before taking the per-author lock, and refuse at once a request older than one already received from that author.
Options that let a lagging voter vote anyway (vote while behind, or within K blocks of its tip) were rejected: since F1-1 the subscriber's fork check assumes that at least f + 1 honest voters matched the leader's block against their own chains.
Pipelined persist (lever 2) and a fix for finding 11 narrow the spread as well.
The bench-6 fields are in §6 (a).

It holds the next round's minimum delay (352 ms), that round's certification (150-155 ms) and delivery.

What dominates at saturation (600 × 16 before the kill, seen p50 14.87 s): the engine queue, S10, 9,566 ms (64 %), plus the subscriber's wait for execution (S7) on the slow executors only (p95 16.9 s; v01 10.7 s p50).
Pre-commit S0-S6a is 2.44 s (16 %), S6b 1.26 s (8 %).
The engine is busy about 1.0 on every node and persist-bound (finding 3); the persist is off the visibility path and reaches seen only through the queue in front of the next outputs.

Little's law holds: own landings per second × mean seen ÷ in flight = 0.97-1.04 on every full generator.
At 600 × 4 the fleet held 24,000 in flight at 4,006 own tx/s with a mean seen of 4.59 s; at 600 × 16, 96,000 at 3,481 tx/s with 23.69 s.
Four times the in-flight transactions gave 13 % less throughput and 5.2 times the mean latency (sampled p50 3.19 → 14.36 s).
At saturation the latency is the client's window divided by the throughput.

The D3 triage adds a caveat and a mechanism. The load window carries the kill, two boundary drains, the starved
generators and the 300-s cliff, so the 13 % is not like-for-like. But consensus commits at the execution rate, so more
in flight means bigger outputs, and the MDBX commit costs more per transaction in a bigger output (29 → 178 µs/tx from
479- to 6,000-transaction outputs, A4n §4): queueing costs capacity through persist.

The arithmetic behind the top five (A6/levers.md "Arithmetic"):
- Lever 1. Measured, not modelled: 600 × 4 ran more throughput than 600 × 16 at a quarter of the latency. Every
  transaction above throughput × floor (about 4,000 tx/s × 1.5 s ≈ 6,000 fleet-wide) only queues, and with F1 the queue
  is the execution backlog. If the node stops admitting new work while the engine is more than about two outputs
  behind (S10 at 600 × 4's 0.4 s), saturated seen becomes commit (3.8 s) + the bound + S11x/S13x (0.44 s) ≈ 4.2-4.7 s:
  −10.2 to −11.7 s at p50 and −29 s at p95, and the slow-executor generators lose their 19-51 s. It adds no capacity;
  in a closed loop the excess waits at the client. It is RPC and pool policy, not consensus-observable.
- Lever 2. The cycle becomes max(execute 479, persist 1,167) = 1,167 ms: throughput × 1,658 ÷ 1,167 = ×1.42 (3,481 → ≈
  4,950 tx/s; the next bound is the commit at 190 µs/tx, ≈ 5,270 tx/s). At fixed in-flight, seen p50 14.87 → 10.5 s and
  p95 35.5 → 25.0 s. It also narrows the execution-lag spread that drives finding 4. Nothing at light load (busy 0.17).
- Lever 3 (corrected by `tasks/issues/bench5-far-validator-certificate-inclusion.md`). A6 priced a re-send at the first
  commit that skips the own certificate at −9.8 to −14 s, assuming each re-proposal had an independent 18 % chance. The
  investigation finds it worth about 0: the far pair's commits come in runs right after each round jump, so a digest
  re-queued while the node lags rides another orphaned header, and an early re-queue could commit a batch twice. The
  re-send variant is dropped. The fix is a parent grace (#1781 variant 2): after 2f + 1 parents, a proposer waits up to
  G = 150 ms for the certificates of validators that were live in the previous round. The RTT-matrix model gives London
  and the Netherlands 100 % committed for G from 100 to 210 ms, so their generators' seen p50 goes from 14.5-18.7 s to 2 s
  or less (−12.5 to −16.7 s). The cost is a longer light-load round, 345 → 364-404 ms (+5 to +16 %), about +0.07 to
  +0.19 s on the fleet's seen p50, and about 0 under load, where rounds are already longer than the far arrival. With
  equal demand per region 20 % of transactions would see 15-19 s today, so this is the largest p95 lever at light load.
- Lever 4 returns three generators and their 28,800 in flight to the throughput (finding 4).
- Lever 5. Committing one certification earlier removes S5-sized to round-sized time from S6b: seen 1.51 → 1.16-1.36 s
  at light load (−10 to −23 %), −0.27 to −0.45 s at 600 × 4, −0.4 s of 14.9 at saturation. The largest pure protocol
  cut of the floor.

Against the design's ranking (A6/levers.md "Against the design's ranking"): below saturation #532 moves to the top of the fleet-median list (S6b is 40 % of the floor), the round-time arm is worth −0.19 s, not −0.3 to −0.5 s, far-validator inclusion is −12.5 to −16.7 s for the users it affects rather than −0.7 s (bench 5 measured orphaning, not a 1-s penalty), L6 has already delivered its part and `max_batch_delay` is worth nothing.
At saturation the in-flight bound is the only lever worth more than ×2 in latency (index 2 is its proof), pipelined persist is the throughput lever, the parallel root adds nothing once persist is pipelined, and the execution-behind starvation of the fastest proposers (finding 4) is a new inclusion failure.

The transaction type does not matter: per-type latency is flat in the landings sample (A6/landings.md §1).

### 6. Far-validator certificate inclusion at light load: certified, then orphaned

Evidence (A2/certification.md "London and Netherlands at light load", A6/stages.md §6, A1/light.md):
- v02 (London) and v06 (Netherlands) certify about 99.5 % of their headers at idle and light load, but only 25.6 / 25.0 %
  (idle), 18.1 / 17.7 % (light8) and 13.3 / 15.3 % (light16) of those certificates are ever committed. Mumbai commits
  86-89 %, every other node 100 %.
- The mechanism (issue text, traced in the code at 067639def): every non-leader proposes exactly `min_header_delay`
  after its own previous header, and the leader of the next even round proposes without waiting (`calc_min_delay`,
  `proposer.rs:559-569`). The eight nearer validators each lead about one round in 16, so the network averages 345.7 ms
  per round. London and the Netherlands never lead, because the reputation table swapped them out of every leader slot
  after their first orphaned certificates; they run at 353.1 ms and lose 7.4 ms per round. Their certificate already
  reaches the Asia-Pacific majority after it has proposed, so no header of the next round lists it, and a certificate can
  be a parent only in the very next round (`proposer.rs:751-760`, `handler.rs:1228`). After about 60 rounds the lag is a
  full round: a quorum of the next round reaches them before their own timer fires, they skip a round (the same round on
  both nodes) and propose at once. That jump cuts their own chain of certificates, so everything since their previous
  commit stays orphaned.
- There is no 20-s timer. Their stranded digests are re-queued at the first own commit after a jump
  (`process_committed_headers`, `proposer.rs:803-858`), which happens at the same round on both nodes, hence the same
  millisecond: v02:16:18:09.329 and v06:16:18:09.339, `digests resent count=1 from_round=373 round=419 cause=committed`,
  36-180 rounds after the orphaned round (p50 58, about 20 s). First proposal → commit p50 13.9 s (light8) and 27 s
  (light16); S5 p50 12.6-17.2 s, p95 40-66 s.
- Their users: seen 14.5-18.7 s against 1.3-2.0 s elsewhere, in synchronized bursts (both silent for the same 41 s from
  16:18:28 in light8: two drift cycles); 8-9 own tx/s against 59-93 for the others, because their senders are
  window-blocked behind the wait.
- Under load the round is set by parent arrival (443-746 ms), not the timer, so nothing drifts: at 600 × 4 76-85 % of
  their certificates commit and their seen is 3.7-3.9 s; under load 99.2-99.7 %.
- Leader share closes the loop: orphaned certificates score 0 in reputation, which keeps them out of the leader slots and
  so off the zero-delay shortcut. v02 led 198 and v06 193 of the 12,834 committed leader rounds, against 1,105-1,677 for the
  others; the same pair was lowest in bench 4 (302, 255). They also log the most `Repropose … undelivered headers` (647 /
  703 against 17-275) (A4n/bottlenecks.md B6).

This is bench 4's idle observation (London and Netherlands certificates 70 % not committed, bench-4 F4) with its user cost now measured.
The fix for bench 6 is the parent grace of lever 3 (G = 150 ms, node-local, cherry-pickable, consensus-observable, so it needs its own design row), with a G = 100 arm; `min_header_delay` 450 is the control arm only (model 65-83 % committed, round +26 %), not the fix.
Weak links to late certificates (a fork-gated protocol change) and any change to reputation or swaps are bench 7+ at the earliest.
Investigation: `tasks/issues/bench5-far-validator-certificate-inclusion.md` (issue text).

### 7. Slow executors, loaded epoch closes that wait for execution, and records at exactly 7 of 10

#### 9. Accepted transactions lost until the client's 300-s resend, and the resends included twice

- The tail has an edge. Of the 82,830 sampled mixed-phase landings of the load, 1,530 (1.85 %) were seen 300 s or more
  after submission and none between 250 and 300 s. They come from 8 of 10 generators (London 287, the Netherlands 251,
  Mumbai 193, Seoul 184, Hong Kong 175, Tokyo 170, Singapore 143, Ashburn 104; Sydney 22, Los Angeles 1), submitted all
  through the load. The light blasts and 600 × 4 have no such tail (p99 15.1 / 18.5 / 6.7 s). This is the run's land p99.
- The 300 s is the generator's gap repair: a sender resends its in-flight transactions unchanged when the chain nonce has
  not moved for 300 s and `eth_getTransactionByHash` on its node returns null for the head-of-line transaction (for
  example blaster-04 at 17:43:40.918, `pinned node lost the head-of-line tx; resending in-flight txs … resend=16`). The
  node had accepted these transactions without error, and then neither executed them nor knew them.
- Where half of them went: 808 of the 1,530 (52.8 %) were skipped at execution before they landed (736 `nonce too high`,
  72 `too low`); a control set of 3,295 transactions seen in 5-20 s matched none. The first case is at the load start,
  v02:17:40:31.237, output 11578, `nonce 298 too high, expected 283`: one sender's batches executed out of nonce order,
  the later nonces were skipped, and since they had left the pool when their batch was sealed nothing brought them back.
  The other 47 % have no skip line; candidates are the starved trio's cycled batches and the batches rejected at the
  epoch change (`BatchEpochMismatch` 5,716, quorum_waiter `Invalid epoch` 6,383), not checked.
- The same gap includes resends twice. All 37,683 `nonce too low` skips per node fall in outputs 12084-12091 at
  17:55:01-17:55:20, the first outputs after the starved trio certified again (output 12084 carried 52,100 transactions).
  The trio's transactions had sat in sealed batches that cycled uncertified for over 10 min; the RPC did not know them, so
  the generators resent them after 300 s, the pool accepted the same bytes again, both copies committed and the second
  was skipped. At batch level, v04's worker rebuilt byte-identical batches 300 s apart, each 40-90 ms after the
  generator's resend (`batch built digest=0x6024039a… num_txs=16 reason=drained pool_age_p50_ms=300187` at
  v04:17:43:41.39, again at 17:48:41.52 and 17:53:41.65). Five batch digests were committed and executed two or three
  times, nine extra blocks.
- Severity: user-visible (a wallet that does not resend never sees its transaction land, and gets no error) and a
  correctness invariant (consensus-safe, because every node skips the same copies, but a transaction skipped as `too
  high` in its first copy can execute in a later one, which changes inclusion order). Nothing dedupes a transaction
  between seal and execution, or a batch digest already sealed, held by the proposer or executed.
- Reproducible on 8 of 10 generators. Bench 4's per-generator p99 sat near 300 s on four generators (read from the
  top histogram bucket) and the earlier runs' land p99 is 443-502 s; whether that is the same cliff is not checked, as
  those runs have no landings sample.
- Investigation: `tasks/issues/bench5-sealed-tx-invisible-drops-and-duplicates.md` (issue text). First questions: why one
  sender's batches commit out of order, whether a skipped transaction should return to the pool (A6 lever 10), and a
  dedupe between seal and execution.

#### 10. The loaded epoch boundary drain

The timings are in finding 7: with F1 a close waits for each node to execute up to the closing output, the new epoch
needs 2f + 1 nodes in, so the gap is the seventh node's drain (22.6 s at epoch 7, 11.0 s at epoch 8), and the record
forms from the first seven (exactly 7 of 10 both times, zero margin with one member down). The triage adds what happens
on the late nodes: all ≈ 1,400 tn::tasks `Failed to track spawned task … no available capacity. The task is already
spawned but untracked: it can never be joined or aborted` errors are on v01, v03 and v05, at 17:56:19-17:56:30 (their
late epoch-8 entry) and at v05's entry after its restart. The same window holds v01's 38 `Invalid epoch` vote failures,
the batch requests turned away at capacity (v01 3,371, v03 1,484), `failed to fetch batches` (1,248 / 680) and all 6,675
open-phase sheds: the new epoch's buffered traffic arrives at once. Reproduced at both loaded closes here, in bench 4
(13 s) and bench 3 (12 / 26 s). Levers: execute ahead to the closing output, pipelined persist, the task tracker's
capacity at epoch entry, and the record's quorum margin. Whether untracked tasks leak across epochs is not checked.
Investigation: `tasks/issues/bench5-loaded-epoch-boundary-drain.md` (issue text).

#### 11. Execution lag feeds itself

The slow executors are a state, not hardware. At light load all ten validators and the observer cost the same (`finish`
4.4-4.9 µs/tx, signer job 12.1-13.4). In the 600 × 4 blast the slow node was v09 (`finish` 18.6, job 21.0 µs/tx,
queue_wait 1,832 ms, the only queue above 657 ms); under the load it was v01, v03 and v05, with v09 in the middle tier.
Across nodes and windows `finish` and the job cost rise with the engine queue: the observer at a 1.6-s queue 8.8 / 12.0
µs/tx, v04 at 3.6 s 10.2 / 13.1, the middle tier at 8.5-9.6 s 11.1-11.6 / 13.0-13.2, the slow three at 12.8 s 17.1-18.7 /
18.5-20.9. Every node is the same machine type. The code path that fits (a hypothesis): each queued output has an E4
signer-recovery job on the global rayon pool, which competes with the rayon work inside the executing output's `finish`,
so a node that starts behind gets 2-3 % slower than the commit rate and its queue only grows. CPU frequency or cache
effects at 17-19 % CPU are the alternative to rule out. This lag spread is what starves the fastest proposers (finding 4)
and makes v01 and v03 the late nodes at both loaded closes (finding 10). Offline test: an engine Criterion run with N
outputs queued against none, and a dedicated recovery pool against the shared one. Investigation:
`tasks/issues/bench5-execution-lag-feedback.md` (issue text).

Needs data (triage): a clean 600 × 16 arm (no chaos, the 300-s cliff fixed), or A4n's 600 × 4 window re-cut on the real
phases, before comparing the two points' capacity (the planned phases add about 81 s of cool-down to A4n's window, D42);
v09's 3 not-found leader rounds at 600 × 4; RSS of 6.3-8.5 GB after the restart, cache or leak; whether bench 3 and
bench 4 had the 300-s cliff.
