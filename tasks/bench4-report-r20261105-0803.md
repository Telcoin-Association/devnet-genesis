# Bench 4 report: r20261105-0803 (main + V1Lazy + observability on v2 nodes, trust-everywhere admission)

Private findings report for review. Run 2026-11-05, segment 1 launched 08:03:32Z, blast 09:08:29Z to 09:53:12Z, segment
3 done 10:21:34Z, teardown 10:22:02Z to 17:19:37Z. Evidence lives under
`benchmark/private/bench4-r20261105-0803/<unit>/` (A1 KPIs, A2 certification and vote path, A3 chaos, recovery and
boundaries, A4n node-side census and bottlenecks, A4o operations) and `benchmark/results/r20261105-0803/`. Node code is
cited as `crates/<path>:line @496ebefb5` (telcoin-network) and harness code as `benchmark/<path>:line @d6a8d53`; every
cited line was checked in a checkout at that sha. Log cites are `<node>:<UTC time>`, where vNN is bench-validator-NN and
o11 the observer.

## 1. Summary

Ten validators in ten GCP regions plus one observer ran telcoin-network 496ebefb5 (origin/main a0d6e6e26 + PR 1783
V1Lazy + nine observability commits, forks `subsecond-from-genesis`) on v2 node scripts with trust-everywhere admission,
from one coordinated start (08:22:35Z, 11/11 in the same second), through a 40-min mixed blast with the standard chaos
plan. No node panicked or logged an MDBX or RPC error, the only engine errors are three on the observer at its chaos
restart, and every epoch closed on time. Throughput rose: 4,578 tx/s with all generators running against 4,037 in
r20261103-1922 (+13.4 %), 5,434 in r20261007-0316 (-15.8 %) and 3,890 in r20261006-0458; 12.31 M landed (+0.8 %),
15.84 blocks/s (+17.6 %), land p50 / p95 3.30 / 12.84 s, p99 31.4 s against 443 s, 7,677 accepted transactions unlanded
against 45,426 (A1/kpis_table.md). Why, one line each:
- The engine's cost per transaction stayed flat at about 215 µs (bench 3: 160 rising to 310 µs), so the 5-min throughput
  series is flat at 4,179-4,829 tx/s instead of falling from 5,986 to 3,045 (A1). Both runs were execution-bound, so this
  is the throughput gain (inference from A4n's saturation and flat per-tx cost; no arm isolates V1Lazy or the debug log)
  (A4n).
- V1Lazy made a vote cost one round trip instead of two: the idle round fell to the 350-ms minimum (352 against 451 ms)
  and London and the Netherlands certify 99.7 / 99.6 % of their idle headers (bench 3: 5.2 / 5.0 %) (A2).
- So the far validators' users now land: London 1.73 M and the Netherlands 1.27 M own transactions (bench 3: 281 k and
  401 k); the worst generator's land p95 is 18.6 s (bench 3: 330.8 s), the run's p99 31 s (bench 3: 443 s) (A1).
The best 60 s fell to 5,525 tx/s (-7.3 %): bench 3's best minute came before its decline; this run has a flat ceiling.

What the eight observability questions (design D2) answered:

| Q | question | answer | finding |
|---|---|---|---|

Two new systemic findings:
1. **Under load a validator whose own consensus commit falls 41 rounds behind is demoted, with no fault and no
   chaos.** v08 (Hong Kong) at 09:20:07 and v07 (Singapore) at 09:27:01, mid-epoch, with no chaos and no committee
   rotation. The chain of cause: a slower disk sync (MDBX commit p50 533 and 420 ms against 305-364 ms on the rest),
   so a longer engine queue (p95 15.3 and 14.5 s against 10.0-11.3 s), so late votes (exec_wait, then the vote lock) and
   an own Bullshark commit that drifted 2-6 s a minute behind the fleet (the consensus loop waits for local execution
   of each leader's block), until `we are behind … reason=outside_gc_window` fired at 41 rounds. That shrank the
   committee to nine, then eight voters; the chaos kill of v05 at 09:33:50 left exactly seven, the quorum, and commits
   stalled for 17 s (bench 3's kill: no stall). Both came back only at an epoch boundary (v08 09:54:54.9, v07 10:14:55.6),
   and their generators landed nothing after 09:20:06 and 09:27:02 (`-32603 validator is inactive`) (A3, A4n, A2).
2. **Execution, not persistence, is now the limit, at about 215 µs per transaction.** The evidence of the bound is the
   engine busy about 0.98 on every node, its queue at the cap of 8, and chain throughput holding while the landing
   generators fell from ten to seven. That busy / 215 µs/tx (about 4.6 k tx/s) matches the measured 4,578 only shows
   that the engine's timings and the chain agree, since both count the same transactions. Execute is 75 % of an output's
   task and its per-tx cost is flat; persist is 24 % and no longer grows (bench-3 F1 half resolved: saturated yes,
   persist growing no). Not separated: this run's `engine=debug` filter formats a whole `ExecutedBlock` per block and
   two lines per transaction on the execution path (A4n, A4o D23).

Verdict: this build is the baseline for the vote path and for a flat per-transaction cost. Before bench 5, fix the
engine log filter (49 GB of unit log per validator, a 4-h log pull in a 7-h teardown, and an unmeasured share of the
execution cost),
and treat the commit-lag demotion (F1) and the promotion loop (F3) as the node defects that set the liveness margin
under load: with three of ten out, one more fault stops the chain.

Mechanism (checked @496ebefb5):
- Demotion: `behind_consensus` (`crates/consensus/primary/src/network/handler.rs:166`) takes `effective_exec_round =
  exec_round.max(processed_consensus_round).max(committed_round)` (`:202`) and demotes when `epoch == exec_epoch &&
  (effective_exec_round + gc_depth) < round` (`:219`), where `gc_depth` there is `gc_activity_window(50)` = 50 - 10 = 40
  (`crates/types/src/primary/mod.rs:64, :72`) and `round` is the incoming certificate's. It logs `we are behind` (`:259`)
  and sends `NodeMode::CvvInactive` (`:261`).
- Coupling of commit to execution: the consensus task handles each certificate in `new_certificate`
  (`crates/consensus/primary/src/consensus/state.rs:583`, Bullshark at `:603`) and, for every committed sub-DAG, waits
  until local execution reaches the leader's `latest_execution_block` (`wait_for_execution`, `:622`) before it sends the
  sub-DAG and publishes the new committed round (`:682`). While it waits it processes no further certificate. All three
  terms of `effective_exec_round` therefore follow the node's own execution, and the engine stops taking outputs at 8
  queued (`crates/engine/src/lib.rs:288`, `MAX_QUEUED_OUTPUTS = 8` at `:55`). A full queue is 8 x 1.8-2.7 s of task time,
  14-22 s of backlog (A4n T6), against a 40-round window that is 30-60 s at the loaded 0.8-1.5-s round.
- Why a small per-node slowdown is enough (inference from A4n and A3 numbers): every engine was saturated (busy
  976-988 ms/s), so no node had spare rate to catch up, and the committee's pace is set by the voters a quorum needs,
  because a voter answers only once its own execution reaches the header's execution block (`handler.rs:1025`). A
  validator whose engine is a few per cent slower than the quorum's falls behind for as long as the load lasts. Task
  cost per transaction: v08 246 µs (8-13 % above the others' 218-227), v07 237 (4-8 %), v04 230 (1-6 %, normal
  persist). The drift rate is not proportional to that (v04: 0.35 s a minute; v07: 2 s; v08: about 6 s); the two that
  were demoted are the two with the slow persist (87 and 68 µs/tx against 55-61), so the persist, and within it the
  fsync, is the likely difference (hypothesis).
- The vote refusals that follow are a consequence: an inactive node's committed round reads 0 in the new consensus
  instance, so its TooNew limit is `committed_round + gc_depth` = 50 (`handler.rs:951`, `vote refused: header too new` at
  `:961`); v08 refused 14,547, v07 19,311 and v05 18,857 vote requests as TooNew (A4n T4).

Impact: two of ten validators left the committee under load with no fault but a slower MDBX fsync, their users could
not submit for 23-30 min, and the committee ran at exactly quorum for 21 min (09:33:50-09:54:55), so the planned kill
became a 17-s stall and one more fault in that window would have stopped the chain. Chain throughput did not fall
(4,433 -> 4,646 tx/s as the landing set shrank), because execution, not voting, sets it (F2). A demoted member returns
only at an epoch entry (F3), so a demotion costs at least the rest of the epoch, and a missed entry a whole further
epoch (v07: 48 min out).

Protocol improvement: demote a validator when it cannot follow consensus, not when its execution is a few per cent
slower than the quorum's; and remove the per-node disk sensitivity of the execution rate.

Patch proposal:
1. Base the activity check on consensus progress. Either let the consensus task commit and publish
   `committed_round` before `wait_for_execution` (`state.rs:615-622`; its own comment anticipates following gossiped
   output), bounded by a queue of committed sub-DAGs awaiting execution, so `committed_round` measures consensus; or
   give `behind_consensus` a consensus-only round (the highest round of certificates the node has accepted) and keep
   the execution lag as a separate, time-based signal.
2. Until 1 lands, a demotion grace: demote on `outside_gc_window` only if the gap has grown over several consecutive
   checks and the executed round is not advancing, and log the engine queue depth and queue_wait in the `we are behind`
   line so the cause is visible.
3. F2's overlap of the fsync of output N with the execution of N+1 takes the fsync (about 0.35 s of every output) off
   the critical path, which is where v08's deficit lives.
4. Harness/infra: a per-node fsync probe at deploy (`fio` with `--fsync=1` on the data volume) recorded in the manifest,
   and a warning when one node is more than 20 % slower than the median; compare the v08 and observer volumes.

Test plan: a `behind_consensus` unit test in which the execution lag is inside the queue bound while consensus is current
(expect no demotion); an e2e test with one validator's persist slowed by 10 % under sustained load (expect it to stay
CvvActive for 20 min). Next run: `tn_node_mode_transitions_total{reason="outside_gc_window"}` on non-chaos validators
(target 0), per-node commit drift per minute, MDBX sync p50 per node, and the committee size at the kill.

### F2. Execution is the throughput limit, at a flat cost of about 215 µs per transaction

Evidence (A4n/bottlenecks.md §1, census-tables.md T5-T7; A1/kpis.md):
- The engine was saturated on all 11 nodes: `rate(tn_engine_busy_milliseconds_total)` 976-988 ms/s in every 5-min
  bucket of load (891 in the warm-up), `tn_engine_queued_outputs` at its cap of 8, queue wait (`output execution task
  finished queue_wait_ms`) 7.3 s p50 and 11.1 s p95 on v01 (T7).
- Split per non-empty output in load (v01, 627 outputs; the others agree within a few per cent, T5): task 1.79 s p50
  (mean 2.08 s), of which execute 1.29 s p50 (75 %), persist 0.49 s p50 / 0.86 s p95 (24 %), announce 11 ms. Inside the
  persist, the MDBX commit is 70 % (357 ms p50) and `save_blocks` 30 % (126 ms); the commit is almost all fsync
  (`reth_database_transaction_commit_sync_duration_seconds` p50 0.33-0.38 s against a write p50 at the 2.5-ms bucket
  floor).
- Flat per transaction (T6, v01 by 5-min bucket): task 185 µs/tx at 09:10, 231 at 09:15, 212 at 09:45; execute 138 ->
  166 µs/tx, flat from 09:15; persist 46 -> 64 -> 45 µs/tx. Outputs grow because rounds slow (0.61 -> 0.33 outputs/s,
  8.1 k -> 12.5 k transactions per output, T6-T7), and each output carries proportionally more work.
- Ceiling: the evidence that execution bounds the chain is busy about 0.98 on every node, the queue at its cap of 8,
  and chain throughput holding while the landing generators fell from ten to seven (F1). That 0.98 / about 215 µs per
  transaction is about 4.6 k tx/s, the measured 4,578 tx/s average (4,179-4,829 per 5 min, A1), only shows that the engine
  log and the chain agree: busy and µs/tx come from the same task times and transactions.
- Against bench 3 (A4n/bottlenecks.md "Bench 3 -> bench 4"):

| | bench 3 (1922) | bench 4 (0803) |
|---|---|---|
| engine busy in load | 1.00 (sub-DAGs/s x mean execution) | 0.976-0.988 (busy counter) |
| per-output split | block span 0.46-0.65 s + persist gap 0.97 -> 1.60 s (persist 60-72 %) | execute 1.05 -> 2.04 s, persist 0.40 -> 0.61 s (persist 21-27 %) |
| engine cost per tx | 160 -> 310 µs, rising | 185 -> 212-231 µs, flat |
| device writes in load | 480 MB/s mean, peaks at the 800 MB/s cap on all nodes | 210-230 -> 150-175 MB/s; peaks above 800 only on v07, v08, o11 |
| bytes written per landed tx (device) | 80 -> 170 KB, rising | 52 -> 37 KB, falling |
| PSI io some | 17 -> 29 % | 6.7-8.6 -> 3.4-4.8 % |
| 5-min chain tx/s | 5,986 -> 3,045 | 4,179-4,829, flat |

- Latency: every landed transaction waits for the queue (7-9 s p50 at 8 outputs deep) plus its own task (1.8-2.7 s).
  That is the 8-s floor of the commit-latency p95 (bench 3: 4.68 s) and most of the 12.84-s land p95 (A1).

Mechanism (checked @496ebefb5):
- One output executes at a time; intake stops at `self.queued.len() < MAX_QUEUED_OUTPUTS` (`crates/engine/src/lib.rs:288`,
  `MAX_QUEUED_OUTPUTS = 8` at `:55`); the task's `queue_wait_ms` / `task_ms` line is at `:179-185`.
- Each output's blocks are built over one shared `output_overlay` (`crates/engine/src/payload_builder.rs:124`), then the
  whole output is persisted once, `persist_output_with_retry` (`:317`) -> `persist_executed_output`
  (`crates/tn-reth/src/env/execution.rs:326`): `save_blocks(…, SaveBlocksMode::Full)` (`:345`) and one
  `provider_rw.commit()` (`:360`). This is origin/main's per-output overlay and retrying persist, which bench 3's build
  lacked; it is consistent with the persist share falling from 60-72 % to 24 % (A4n). The `output executed` line with
  `execute_ms persist_ms announce_ms` is at `payload_builder.rs:340-350`.
- The commit path and the vote path both wait on this engine (`state.rs:622`, `handler.rs:1025`), which is how the
  execution queue becomes commit lag (F1) and voter wait (F5).

Not separated: the run's `engine=debug` filter formats, on the executing thread, a Debug dump of the whole
`ConsensusOutput` per output (`executing output`, `payload_builder.rs:74`, about 18.7 MB a line), of the whole
`ExecutedBlock` per block (`block executed`, `:372`, about 716 kB a line) and two lines per transaction (`governance
collects`, `allocating basefees`, `crates/tn-reth/src/evm/handler.rs:132, :181`). Together they wrote 1,160 MB a minute
per validator under load (A4o D23). How much of the 165 µs/tx execute time that costs is not measured; it is the first
thing bench 5 settles. The workload confound A4n raised for bench 3 (contract-heavy mix) applies to both runs equally:
same generator sha, same 13-type mix.

Impact: the engine sets the chain's rate (about 4.6 k tx/s here) and adds 9-12 s to every transaction. The same
saturation leaves no node any spare rate, which is why a node whose engine is about 10 % slower per tx (its fsync about
55 % slower) is demoted (F1), why voters wait (F5), why loaded boundaries cost 13 s (F7) and why a follower under load
does not gain on the chain (F3). Bench-3 F1 is half resolved: still saturated, but the persist no longer grows and the
per-transaction cost is flat.

Protocol improvement: more execution throughput per output, and the fsync off the critical path.

## 6. Open questions and the next runs

What to change before bench 5, in order:
1. **The engine log filter** (D23, A4o A1). Keep `output execution task finished`, `output executed` and `persisted
   output`, drop the `ExecutedBlock` / `ConsensusOutput` dumps and the per-transaction lines, and check one 5-min loaded

   more than 20 % off the median. Open: is this per-volume variance of the disk type, and would a different type (pd-ssd,
   hyperdisk-extreme) or a machine shape with local SSD remove it? A probe on a fresh fleet answers the first part.

   ceiling for longer than a few minutes.

   the current build cleanly and bench 6 the fix.
