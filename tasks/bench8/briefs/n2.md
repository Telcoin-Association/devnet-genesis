# N2 — certified share of under-held headers against the wait of the lagging voters

Common brief: `tasks/bench8/briefs/n-common.md`. Output `benchmark/private/bench8-prep/N2/`. Checkpoint `tasks/checkpoint-bench8-N2.md`.

Question: an under-held header is certified when enough lagging voters reach the named block inside their wait (`VOTE_EXECUTION_WAIT`, 1 s, before the author lock) or before the author gives up. Does the certified share of under-held headers follow one curve against how long the lagging voters had to wait, in light blast 1 and in the load blast alike?

Sections:
1. Per under-held header (runs 7a and 7b; light blast 1, the load blast before the kill, 7b's first 817 s): the wait the 7th holder needed (the time from the request's arrival at the voter that completes a quorum of holders until that voter's tip reached the named block; from `votes[v]` and the tip series, as the data map describes), and whether the header was certified.
2. The curve: certified share by wait bucket (0-100 ms, 100-250, 250-500, 500-1,000, over 1,000 ms), per window and run, with counts. State the bucket counts; a bucket under 30 headers is marked thin.
3. What the voters answered on lost under-held headers: counts by outcome (`ExecutionBehind`, timeout, a vote that came after the author moved on, `missing_parents`, other), per run.
4. The headers that were NOT under-held and still lost (7a 102, 7b 271): the same outcome counts. 7b's share is twice 7a's: what differs?

Decides: one curve for both blasts → the loss is the execution lag meeting the 1-s wait, and a deeper floor (run F) or a longer wait would act on it; two different curves → something besides the wait acts in the load blast (run H or a voter-wait parameter moves up).
