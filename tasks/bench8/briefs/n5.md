# N5 — the far validators' last step: do near headers of the next round name the late certificate?

Common brief: `tasks/bench8/briefs/n-common.md`. Output `benchmark/private/bench8-prep/N5/`. Checkpoint `tasks/checkpoint-bench8-N5.md`.

Background: the bench 7 report (§4.5) and the posted issue telcoin-network #1870 say that a far validator's header (validators 02 and 06) is certified, yet often never committed, and INFER the last step: the near validators' round r+1 headers are built before the far certificate of round r reaches them, so they do not name it as a parent. Parents are not logged, only their count (`num_parents` on `header proposed`).

The test the count allows: a committee of ten has at most ten certificates in round r. A round r+1 header with `num_parents = 10` names every one of them, the far validator's too. If such a header was itself committed, the far certificate was in a committed header's history and must have been committed with it.

Sections:
1. From the data map: where `num_parents`, the certified and committed marks per header (author, round), and `certificate committed` / `subdag committed` lines are. Build, per run (7a and 7b; light blasts and the load blast before the kill), the list of far certificates (author 02 or 06, round r) that were certified and never committed.
2. For each such (far author, r): the near authors' round r+1 headers: how many exist, their `num_parents` distribution, how many have 10, and how many of those were committed.
3. The verdict table: uncommitted far certificates for which at least one committed round r+1 header had 10 parents (the inference is FALSE for those: say how many, and look at three by hand: maybe the round had fewer than ten certificates, so 10 parents is impossible; maybe the far header was certified only after round r+1 was built, which the certificate time shows); those for which no round r+1 header had 10 parents (consistent with the inference); those with no data.
4. How many certificates round r had at all (count certified headers of round r): `num_parents` equal to that count means "named all there were".
5. Timing where the data has it: the far certificate's formation time (the author's `quorum reached` or certificate line) against the creation time of the near round r+1 headers (`created_at_ms`). How often did the far certificate form AFTER the first near r+1 header was created?

Decides: if committed ten-parent headers follow uncommitted far certificates, the inferred step is false and #1870's text needs a correction; if not, the inference stands as far as the count can show, and bench 8's `parents` field settles it.
