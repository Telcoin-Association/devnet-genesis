## Why
At saturation in bench 5 (r20261107-1504) a transaction was seen in a block a median of 14 s after it was sent, and almost all of
that was time queued in front of the execution engine: consensus ordered faster than the engine could execute and persist, so
outputs piled up. Four times the transactions in flight gave no more throughput. An admission bound refuses new transactions at
the RPC while execution is more than K outputs behind, so that the queue is short and the delay is visible to the sender as a
refusal it can retry, not as seconds of silent waiting. It is an operator policy, off by default, and bench 6 measures it in its
own arm, only together with the recovery pool and the tracker drain.

## Deliver (behind `rpc_admission_backlog_outputs`; 0 = off = today's path with no extra work per request)
1. In `run_epoch.rs`: after the send of a consensus output to the engine has succeeded, `self.execution_backlog.record_forwarded(n)`
   (never before the send, never on a failed send), and set the gauge `tn_node_execution_backlog_outputs` to
   `outputs_behind()` there. The gauge is updated with the switch on or off (it is a measurement).
2. In the RPC guard: one more check before the transaction is decoded: if the gate does not admit, refuse with the pool-overflow
   error the RPC already has (`EthApiError::PoolError(RpcPoolError::TxPoolOverflow)`, whose text is "txpool is full"), and count
   `tn_reth_rpc_admission_refused_total`. One `AdmissionGate` per RPC server, built from the env's backlog handle and limit;
   `admits()` must cost one or two atomic reads on the request path.
3. What is NOT gated, and must be shown by a test or stated with the reason: transactions an observer forwards to a validator
   (the forward path), the re-pooling of an orphaned batch's transactions at an epoch close, and anything that is not a raw
   transaction submission.
4. State in the hand-back what the bound counts (outputs queued + executing + persisting, from forwarded minus executed) and what
   a sender sees at K = 2 under saturation (how long the gate stays closed per cycle), from the code, not from a guess.
