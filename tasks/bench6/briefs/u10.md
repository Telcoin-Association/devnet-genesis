## Part A — the proposer does not queue a digest it already holds

### Why
In bench 5 (r20261107-1504) five batch digests reached headers more than once, a batch whose report failed at an epoch boundary
was rebuilt with the same digest about 85 times, and resent transactions executed twice (37,683 `nonce too low` skips). Unit U7
made the worker skip a digest it had already reported successfully. The proposer is the second place a duplicate can enter: a
digest reported to it twice is queued twice and can ride two headers.

### Tests

is dropped when reported again; the re-queue of a cancelled header's digests is not treated as a duplicate and the digests ride
the next header; the ack is sent in every case; switch off → both reports are queued as today.

## Part B — the digest wait must not restart on a re-queue

A reviewer noted on upstream PR #1819 that the proposer's digest-wait measurement restarts when a digest is re-queued (its
`received` stamp is reset: near :80-88 and :392 before the other units' edits), so the histogram under-reports exactly the
digests that waited longest. The follow-up only documented it and added a `requeued` flag. Fix the measurement, no behaviour
change and no switch: keep a `first_received` stamp that survives a re-queue, record the existing digest-wait histogram from it,
and add `oldest_requeue_wait_ms` to the `header proposed` line (the longest wait, from first receipt, among the re-queued
digests the header carries; 0 when it carries none). Keep every existing field. Tests: a digest re-queued once reports the wait
from its first receipt; a header with no re-queued digest reports 0.
